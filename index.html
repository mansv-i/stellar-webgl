Astrophysics & High-Performance GPU Architecture

1. Dark Matter Halos & Non-Keplerian Kinematics In our Solar System, planets follow Newtonian physics ($v \propto r^{-1/2}$), orbiting slower with distance. Real spiral galaxies defy this: stars at the edge orbit just as fast as stars near the center. This simulation implements Vera Rubin's flat rotation curve, reflecting the gravitational influence of invisible dark matter halos.
2. Lin-Shu Density Wave Dynamics Spiral arms are not physical objects tied to stars. If stars were locked to their arms, differential rotation would tightly wind them up until they vanished within two galactic years (the "winding dilemma"). Arms are acoustic compression density waves that stars orbit through like highway traffic bottlenecks.

Single-Pass GPU Transform Shaders Rather than keeping 250,000 objects in JavaScript memory and looping every frame (which would lock the CPU thread), every star is an indexed mathematical seed computed directly inside a GLSL vertex shader at 60 FPS, utilizing zero megabytes of runtime geometry buffers.
4. Rayleigh-Taylor Hydrodynamic Detonations Thermonuclear events in interstellar space expand across three phases: an initial prompt ionizing X-ray/UV thermal flash, an outward supersonic Mach compression shockwave, and a turbulent buoyant mushroom plume driven by Rayleigh-Taylor convection.
